# Personas

Three personas shaped every interface decision. Each is defined by what they **refuse** to do today.

## 1 · The cautious delegator — "Ana"

Uses AI assistants daily for drafts and research; refuses to let one touch money. Not crypto-native; has tapped
"Allow" on ten thousand app prompts. **Refusal:** "I'm not giving a bot my card." **What wins her:** a cap she sets
herself, a Fire button that visibly works, and never seeing the word blockchain. **Designed for her:** the
permission sheet's "you can lower this", plain hire/fire vocabulary, browse-without-wallet, and the "Blocked before
it happened" moment that turns the guarantee into something she can watch.

## 2 · The agent builder — "Dev"

Ships a subscription-manager bot; conversion dies at the credentials step. **Refusal:** won't build per-platform
trust integrations. **What wins him:** a manifest file that declares what his agent asks for, a credential instead
of stored user secrets (his liability shrinks), and a track record that accrues as receipts he couldn't fake — his
marketing page writes itself. **Designed for him:** the agent detail page ("what it asks for" / "what it can never
do"), receipts-not-reviews, and the Wave-2 listing SDK.

## 3 · The verifying merchant — "Mercado"

An online checkout deciding whether to accept agent-initiated purchases. **Refusal:** won't store more PII, won't
eat chargebacks from "my bot did it" disputes. **What wins them:** a receipt lookup that proves the action was
authorized by a live, funded mandate — with no personal data to secure and cryptographic evidence the human approved
the class of purchase. **Designed for them:** the receipt anatomy view ("what this receipt contains / what it
deliberately omits") and the verifier flow.

## Anti-persona

**The yield tourist.** There is no token, no APY, nothing to farm. Designing consciously against this persona kept
the product honest: every screen answers "what can my agent do," never "what's the price."
